Connecting Latent Student Skills with Immediate Campus Needs

College students frequently require peer assistance—from urgent lab equipment and course tutoring to hackathon teammates—yet requests remain buried in chaotic 1,000-member WhatsApp chats. NeedHub applies supervised classification, regression modeling, and unsupervised clustering to structure peer skill discovery, predict micro-gig willingness, and eliminate dorm asset idleness.

Identified Marketplace Frictions

  • 01
    Discovery Asymmetry: 48.0% of students cannot locate peers who possess the specific skill (e.g., Python tutoring, drafters, UI design) they need immediately.
  • 02
    Pricing Hesitation: 35.6% of capable student providers refrain from offering services because they lack market benchmark rates.
  • 03
    Escrow & Fulfillment Uncertainty: While peer trust is high, lack of micro-payment escrow causes hesitation for equipment borrowing.

How Machine Learning Resolves These Gaps

  • 01
    Supervised Classification: Identifies students with high micro-gig earning propensity (Random Forest achieves 91.7% Recall), ensuring proactive onboarding of campus suppliers.
  • 02
    Continuous Regression: Forecasts campus need intensity (Ridge MAE: 3.143) to anticipate exam crunch demands and buffer peer tutor availability.
  • 03
    Unsupervised Segmentation: $k$-Means clustering categorizes students into 4 actionable personas to personalize onboarding nudges and pricing recommendations.

Why Recall is the Essential Metric for NeedHub

In a cold-start peer marketplace, False Negatives are fatal: incorrectly predicting that an eager student will not offer gigs starves the platform of supply inventory. Random Forest achieves 91.7% Recall, capturing 22 out of 24 real giggers on unseen test data.
